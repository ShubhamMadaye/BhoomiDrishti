# Existing System Study — Land Acquisition Monitoring in Infrastructure Governance
**Document Reference:** BhoomiDrishti / SIH26017

---

## 1. Context & Motivation

Land acquisition represents one of the most operationally challenging phases in infrastructure project lifecycles (highways, railways, energy, industrial corridors, and urban transit). Delays during acquisition cascade directly into compounded contract revisions, idle capital, litigation expenses, and delayed public utility.

---

## 2. Analysis of Current Monitoring Paradigms

Traditional land acquisition tracking mechanisms exhibit key structural limitations:

| Dimension | Traditional Monitoring Systems | BhoomiDrishti Predictive Platform |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operational Posture** | **Reactive**: Issues are discovered weeks or months after milestone deadlines have already been missed. | **Proactive**: Early probability estimation flags high-risk projects before deadlines lapse. |
| **Analytics Capability** | Descriptive summaries, historical milestone tables, and static status spreadsheets. | Calibrated ML delay classification, probabilistic risk scoring, and regression horizon modeling. |
| **Transparency & Reasoning**| Opaque subjective officer impressions without quantitative causality signals. | Explainable AI (SHAP attributions) identifying exact operational risk drivers. |
| **Actionability** | Generic review meetings with ad-hoc instructions recorded in static minutes. | Automated, rule-driven recommendations assigned directly to specific officer roles. |
| **Field Integration** | Manual paper reports, physical registers, or disconnected batch data entry. | Mobile field intelligence with offline synchronization and geo-tagged evidence. |
| **Auditability** | Patchy audit logs; difficulty tracing how field progress impacts overall risk. | Immutable, time-stamped audit logs with automated $\Delta \text{Risk}$ recalculation. |

---

## 3. Structural Vulnerabilities Addressed

1. **Information Asymmetry**: State and National authorities often lack real-time visibility into micro-level bottlenecks occurring at the district sub-divisional level.
2. **Delayed Intervention Window**: When an intervention is delayed by 6 months, land price escalation and dispute entrenchment make resolution exponentially harder.
3. **Absence of Feedback Loops**: Interventions assigned in meetings often lack direct verification and outcome tracking back into monitoring systems.
